# Week 3

**Dates:** 06-29 to 07-06

## Goals
My main goal for this week was to conduct a third literature review on the framework associated with dynamic quantum systems rather than focusing primarily on entirely non-classical quantum systems, as previous literature did. A subsequent goal was to begin to consider manners of expanding the current barrier certificate frameworks in order to accomodate the conditional based transitions functions associated with hybrid (classical-quantum) systems/algorithms.

## Approach and Implementation
The approach that I took was to reexamine the fundamental assumptions of barrier certificates in order to understand how these assumptions needed to either be altered or maintained in the presence of more complicated systems. Additionally, I realized that I needed a stronger foundational understanding of quantum mechanics in order to correctly know how to encapsulate quantum behaviour in a more expansive mathematical framework, particularly the kinds operations and the extend to the complexity of operations on the basis of measurement and classical decisions. My work on this led me to develop a preliminary sketch of the desired functionality of the framework, along with what I considered to be a naive solution to the problem of expanding barrier certificates.

## Results
The result of my efforts was the creation of a basic proposal in which I laid out my understanding of the problem of certifying hybrid quantum-classical systems and then my broad solution to the problem. I was able to meet with Prof. Lei and he approved the new framework, although in our meeting I noticed that I still had a somewhat uncertain understanding of the best way of presenting hybrid systems (due to issues of possible errors in gate applications). The source of the issue was that quantum gates can be seen as functions from one state to another, but in the context of physical real world systems, there are certain errors in their applications that a expansive framework should be able to describe. I did not know, at that time how to resolve this issue. Prof. Lei also recommended that over the course of the next week or two I apply my proposed solution to a small 2-qubit system that could not be verified by previous barrier methods.
